Research

We study how nanostructure, material response, and symmetry can be used to control thermal radiation. Our work combines electromagnetic and thermal modeling, nanophotonic design, and experiments to connect fundamental light–matter interactions with energy and thermal technologies.

Symmetry-controlled thermal radiation

We investigate how mirror, rotational, and translational symmetries determine the spectrum, direction, and polarization of thermal emission. By deliberately breaking selected symmetries, we seek metasurfaces that produce directional, polarization-selective, chiral, and other unconventional emissive responses.

Our approach combines full-wave simulation, modal analysis, and coupled-mode theory to connect changes in geometry with changes in radiative coupling and observable thermal emission.

Active infrared metasurfaces

Active metasurfaces provide dynamic control that cannot be achieved with static nanostructures. We study phase-change materials, graphene, and related tunable media for modulating infrared absorption and emission in real time.

Current questions include how material loss, resonance linewidth, and symmetry can be jointly engineered to create reconfigurable, angle-robust, and spectrally selective infrared devices.

Thermal-radiation energy systems

We develop wavelength-selective materials for thermophotovoltaic energy conversion, waste-heat recovery, radiative cooling, and passive thermal regulation. These systems require control over both useful emission bands and unwanted radiative losses.

Our research links nanophotonic design with realistic material properties, temperature-dependent performance, and experimentally accessible structures.

Thermal management and intelligent energy systems

We apply heat-transfer modeling and experiments to building energy systems, battery thermal management, and adaptive thermal control. This work includes reduced-order thermal models, sensor-informed digital twins, and control strategies for improving energy efficiency.

These projects connect fundamental thermal science with deployable technologies and provide accessible research opportunities for graduate, undergraduate, and high-school students.
