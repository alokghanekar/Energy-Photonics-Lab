Collaborate with us

If you are interested in exploring a problem together, whether you want to understand the thermal behavior of a system, learn how a surface emits or absorbs infrared radiation, explore metasurfaces and nanostructures for controlling light-matter interactions, or see how these effects influence performance, we can help with modeling, experiments, design, and early-stage feasibility studies.

Ways to work with us

Measurement and characterization

We measure spectral reflectance and transmittance from 700 nm to 15 µm, at incident angles from 5° to 75°, with independent control of incident and analyzed polarization so co- and cross-polarized components are separated, and extract spectral emissivity for opaque samples. We also measure thin-film thickness and effective optical constants.

SBIR and STTR partnership

The Energy Photonics Lab can serve as the research institution partner on STTR proposals. STTR requires a small business to team with a research institution that performs a defined share of the work.

Our capabilities map onto solicitation topics in infrared signature management, thermal management of electronics and batteries, thermophotovoltaic and waste-heat conversion, and energy-efficient building envelopes, primarily DoD, DOE, and NSF.

Maryland Industrial Partnerships (MIPS)

The Maryland Industrial Partnerships program provides matching funds for university research that helps a Maryland company develop a product, and UMBC is an eligible institution. Projects are proposed jointly by the company and the faculty researcher.

For a Maryland start-up this is the lowest-cost entry point into a substantial project: the program has historically awarded up to $90,000 per year to start-up firms against a much smaller company contribution.

Thermal simulation and modeling

Conjugate heat transfer and CFD

COMSOL Multiphysics with the Heat Transfer and CFD modules, applied to electronics and power-module cooling, battery pack thermal behavior, cold plates and heat exchangers, enclosure and duct flow, and airflow in occupied spaces. Conduction, convection, and radiation are solved together.

HVAC and building energy systems

Zone-level thermal modeling, equipment sizing and load analysis, and evaluation of control strategies. We build reduced-order resistance–capacitance models identified from measured building data, fast enough to run inside a controller or across a full year of weather.

We also work on model-predictive and learning-based control for HVAC, and validate strategies on hardware-in-the-loop testbeds rather than only in simulation.

Thermal design review

An independent look at a thermal design that is not meeting target: where the dominant resistance actually sits, whether the limit is conduction, convection, or radiation, and what the achievable ceiling is for the given form factor and materials.

Often the most valuable outcome is a negative one — establishing early that a passive approach cannot close the gap, before a team spends a design cycle finding out.

Model validation against measurement

Simulation is only as good as the surface properties fed into it, and assumed emissivity values are a common source of error. Because we can measure the actual spectral and angular emissivity of your materials, we can close the loop: measure the surface, put the measured properties into the thermal model, and compare against a physical test.

What we can do for you

  • Design — wavelength-, angle-, and polarization-selective infrared emitters and absorbers, specified to a target spectrum rather than chosen from a catalogue.
  • Model — conjugate heat transfer and CFD, full-wave electromagnetic simulation, and reduced-order thermal models that predict system-level performance rather than component behavior in isolation.
  • Measure — infrared spectral and angle-resolved characterization of your material or coating from the solar band through the long-wave infrared, with electron microscopy to verify that what was fabricated is what was designed.
  • Evaluate — independent assessment of whether a proposed surface treatment, coating, or emitter can meet a performance target, and what the physical limits are.

The underlying reason this is tractable: emissivity is set by resonance linewidth and radiative coupling, not only by a material's bulk absorption. That means a target emission spectrum and angular pattern can be engineered through geometry, largely independently of the intrinsic properties of the material you are constrained to use.

Application areas we work in

Thermal signature management

Tunable and static infrared metasurfaces for controlling apparent temperature and emission direction across the atmospheric transmission windows.

Waste-heat and thermophotovoltaic conversion

Wavelength-selective emitters matched to a photovoltaic cell bandgap, with suppression of sub-bandgap radiative loss.

Radiative cooling and building envelopes

Passive and switchable radiative cooling surfaces, thermochromic coatings, and envelope materials evaluated under realistic conditions.

Electronics and battery thermal management

Conjugate heat transfer simulation, radiative and passive thermal pathways, and sensor-informed control for systems that are thermally limited.

HVAC and building energy systems

Load and equipment analysis, reduced-order zone models identified from measured data, and model-predictive or learning-based control evaluated on hardware-in-the-loop testbeds.

Data centers and power electronics

Airflow and cold-plate modeling, enclosure thermal design, and identification of where the dominant thermal resistance actually sits in a densely packed system.

How to start

Email {{ site.email }} with the subject line beginning [Collaboration]. To make the first reply useful, include:

  • What the device has to do, stated as a target if you have one — a junction or surface temperature limit and heat load for thermal work, or a wavelength band, emissivity level, and angular requirement for optical work.
  • Any constraints you cannot change — substrate, existing coating, area, cost per unit, manufacturing process.
  • Which route you are interested in: simulation, measurement, sponsored research, an SBIR/STTR teaming arrangement, or MIPS.
  • Your timeline, including any proposal deadline.

We reply to well-scoped inquiries within a few business days. If the work is a poor fit for us, we will say so and, where we can, point you to a group that fits better.
